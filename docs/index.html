📓 Journaling Mood & Burnout Tracker

A journaling companion scores the emotion in every sentence with a fine-tuned transformer and asks, per person: has their mood dropped below their own normal and stayed there? The deliverable is a gentle check-in flag, so the evaluation is per journaler — not per sentence — and it asks how much sentence accuracy actually buys.
Web · Streamlit Fine-tuned MiniLM · TF-IDF · lexicon Real public sentences, synthetic lives Personal-baseline CUSUM Screening aid, not a diagnosis
Not a medical device. A research demonstration of a mood-trend screening aid. It does not diagnose burnout, depression or any condition and must not be used to make decisions about a real person. Every journaler is synthetic; the sentences are public social-media text, not diaries. No patient, therapy or crisis-line data is used.

The problem

Journaling apps increasingly offer "mood insights". The tempting build is a sentiment classifier and a chart. The useful question is different: a single bad week is normal and nobody should be flagged for one, but a slow slide that does not lift is worth a gentle, consented check-in. That makes the unit of evaluation the person over months — how many declines are caught, how late, and how many people who were fine get flagged anyway — and it makes sentence accuracy an input whose value has to be measured rather than assumed.

Data: real sentences, synthetic lives

  • dair-ai/emotion (Saravia et al., 2018): English tweets labelled with six emotions by hashtag distant supervision — 16,000 train / 2,000 validation / 2,000 test. Research/educational use.
  • GoEmotions (Demszky et al., 2020, Apache-2.0): Reddit comments rated for 27 emotions; 5,803 single-label comments mapped onto the six classes via the paper's Ekman grouping. Used only as a register-shift test.
  • Validation and test sentences become the pools diaries are written from, after dropping any whose text also appears in training (5 and 11 duplicates removed).
  • 300 synthetic journalers per cohort × 180 days, three cohorts (calibration, evaluation, and the evaluation people rewritten in Reddit sentences). Each has a personal typical mood, day-to-day wobble (AR(1), φ = 0.4), bad weeks, and for 40% a sustained decline starting between day 60 and 130. A bad week is exactly as deep as a decline; only its duration differs.
  • People write on 51% of days, ~4.0 sentences per entry. Whether they write and which emotion each sentence carries depends on mood alone — the generator never sees the label, and a test requires byte-identical diaries when the label is swapped.

Approach

Sentence scorers (trained on tweets only): a fixed keyword lexicon; TF-IDF + logistic regression; frozen all-MiniLM-L6-v2 embeddings + logistic regression; and MiniLM fine-tuned end-to-end on CPU (the shipped scorer, declared before any detection result). Daily score = mean sentence valence, P(joy)+P(love) − P(sadness)−P(anger)−P(fear). Tracker: a personal baseline from the first 35 days, then a one-sided CUSUM of the standardised drop (allowance k = 0.25, chosen on the calibration cohort). Threshold: the quantile of per-person maxima that flags 10% of never-declining people in the calibration cohort, applied unchanged to evaluation. A flag counts as a detection only between onset and six weeks after the decline completes.

2 · Sentence accuracy is not the bottleneck

Across the four scorers, sentence accuracy spans 40 points (best: minilm_ft, 92.8%); detection spans 10 points (best: minilm_ft, 22%). Averaging several sentences over several weeks forgives a great deal of per-sentence error, because the tracker only needs the sign of the mood mass, not the emotion.

The bootstrap 95% interval on the shipped detection rate is ±7 points. The fine-tuned transformer and TF-IDF differ by +5.6 points of sentence accuracy and +2.4 points of detection — inside that interval.

A population threshold ("recent average below X") catches 23% of declines against 22% for the personal CUSUM, and 94% of its false flags land on the gloomiest quarter of people who never declined (personal CUSUM: 14%). Among never-declining people the population rule flags, 50% were within a week of a bad week; for the CUSUM, 14% (base rate of such days: 11%).

Who gets caught differs too. Personal CUSUM: 32% of declines among the more cheerful half of people vs 13% among the gloomier half; population threshold: 10% vs 37%. Part of the personal rule's tilt is built into this generator: word choice follows mood through a logistic link, so a decline that starts from an already-low mood changes the text less. That is an assumption of the simulation, not an observation about people.

A personal baseline is itself an estimate. It rests on a median of 20 entries. Among people who never declined, how much happier their true mood was during the baseline weeks than afterwards — something the tracker never sees — predicts being flagged with AUC 0.79 (mean gap +0.22 for the flagged vs -0.01 for the rest, latent units); having fewer baseline entries predicts it with AUC 0.44. Comparing people with themselves removes who they are, and adds how their first five weeks happened to go.

4 · The people who most need noticing write the least

Once a decline is complete, people write on 33% of days vs 54% for people who never declined. On the days they do write their mood averages -0.85 below normal; across all days it is -1.10 — silence hides the worst days.

Holding every mood and every sentence's emotion fixed and changing only writing behaviour — shipped: 22% caught; writing independent of mood: 38% caught, 11% of never-declining flagged; strongly withdraw when low: 14% caught, 13% of never-declining flagged; write MORE when low: 38% caught, 7% of never-declining flagged. Silence that follows mood costs 15 points of detection; replacing gold labels with the classifier cost 2.

5 · Register shift: same people, Reddit sentences

The evaluation people are rewritten with Reddit comments carrying the same emotion in the same sentence slot. Thresholds stay as fitted on tweets. For the shipped scorer, the share of never-declining people flagged moves by -1 points under the personal CUSUM and by -9 points under the population threshold.

Why. A sentence carrying the same emotion moves the minilm_ft score less on Reddit: the gap between the mean valence of positive- and negative-emotion sentences is 1.93 on tweets and 0.92 on Reddit (gold labels: 2.00 by construction). The population rule's tweet-calibrated cut-point is then rarely reached: it catches 0.8% and flags 0.6% of never-declining people. The personal CUSUM re-standardises each person: its false-flag rate moves from 8.0% to 6.9%, and detection from 22.4% to 10.4%. A personal baseline protected the false-flag rate from the register shift; it could not protect sensitivity, because the signal it standardises had shrunk.

The shipped setting (coupling 1.0) gives a within-person correlation of 0.64 between an entry's gold-label score and true mood. That is an assumption, and a generous one; it is why this table exists.

Across within-person correlations from 0.44 to 0.77, gold-label detection moves between 21% and 25%, against 46% for true mood on the same written days. Past a modest coupling, how clearly words carry mood stops mattering; when people write, and how much a few weeks of text can distinguish a decline from a bad week, limit the tracker.

Does the budget travel? Calibrating on one cohort and evaluating on the other flags 8.0% of never-declining people; swapping the roles gives 13.4%, both against a 10% target. Rewriting the same people from disjoint halves of the sentence pool moves detection between 22% and 27%. At an assumed 10% prevalence, 24% of flags would be real; the in-study 40% would flatter that figure.

Negative controls. A random scorer catches 4.8%; shuffling each person's monitored days (same entries, no sustained shape) catches 16.0%.

Honest limits

  • Tweets and Reddit comments are not diaries, and the lives are simulated. The mood→word coupling, writing behaviour and decline shapes are assumptions; the ones that matter are swept.
  • "Burnout" here is a sustained drop in expressed mood. Burnout proper is a syndrome — exhaustion, cynicism, reduced efficacy — that no sentiment score measures.
  • dair-ai labels are hashtag-derived and keyword-heavy; in-domain accuracy overstates what any of these scorers would do on real journal prose.
  • Sentences are reused across synthetic people, so person-level bootstrap intervals understate classifier-error uncertainty; the pool-replicate spread is reported for that reason.
  • Nothing here has been tested with real users. A flag should only ever open a supportive, consented conversation; it is not a clinical signal.
